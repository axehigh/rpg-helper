# The Root of All Sorrow

*A tabletop RPG adventure for 4 players, level 4, lasting 4–5 hours.*

---

## The Hook

A letter in a shaking hand arrives with the last courier to cross the fens: Sister Aelwen, village midwife and an old friend of one of the heroes, begs the party to reach Thornhollow before the full moon.

*"They mean to choose this year. Not the dying — the young. When the bell rings on the full moon, the tree will take them. Come quickly, and do not eat the black apples."*

No traveler has returned from Thornhollow in years. The village's only road is a rutted lane through black-apple groves, and no one who has worn the grey lichen ring has ever left it. The party must cross the fens, reach the village before the moon swells full, and stop the tree from being fed.

## The Party's Objectives

- Reach Thornhollow before the full moon and learn what the elders feed the tree beneath the well-chapel.
- Follow the crypt-bell through the chapel and find Sister Aelwen among the rooted.
- Descend the sap galleries to the First Root and confront the Root-Keeper.
- Choose the fate of the Root — and of Thornhollow.

---

## The Storyline

### 1. The Black-Apple Road
**Setting:** The fen crossing and the black-apple hedgerows leading to Thornhollow.  
**Battlemap:** `ttlb-p3-l-country-lane` (Country Lane)  
**Beat:** The party crosses a drowned hamlet and meets Benle, a mute youth who fled Thornhollow a year ago and draws roots in the mud. The road's own dead guard the groves. The chapel bell tolls once for each hero — the village knows they are coming.  
**Enemies:**
*   `marsh-hound` x2 (Existing, Beast — re-flavored as the orchard's root-hounds)
*   `marsh-hound` x1 (Existing, Optional wave)

**Tactics:** The root-hounds use Pack Hunt to drag a grappled hero toward the nearest black-apple tree, where the roots are hungry. They do not chase beyond the grove line.  
**Aftermath:** A torn corpse by the drowned mill bears a midwife's badge — Aelwen's — or planted by the tree's servants. Benle draws the well-chapel in the mud and touches his wrist where the lichen ring would be.  
**Read Aloud:**
The road into the valley narrows between hedgerows heavy with black apples, each fruit swollen and glossy, speckled with grey. Where the hedgerows break, you pass a drowned hamlet — a millwheel still turning in a pond that smells of copper, a bell-crier's house with its clapper stolen. At the foot of a great tree that has split a mass grave open, a boy sits cross-legged, drawing in the mud. He does not look up when you approach. He is drawing a root, over and over. Behind him, from the direction of Thornhollow, a bell begins to toll — once for each of you — and falls silent, as if it has counted you.

**Read Aloud (Norwegian):**
Veien inn i dalen smalner mellom hekker som er tunge av svarte epler — hvert frukt hovent og blankt, prikket med grått. Der hekkene brytes, passerer dere en druknet landsby: et møllehjul som fremdeles går rundt i en dam som lukter kobber, og et ringerves hus med klokken stjålet. Ved foten av et stort tre som har splittet en massegrav åpen, sitter en gutt med korslagte ben og tegner i gjørmen. Han ser ikke opp når dere nærmer dere. Han tegner en rot, om og om igjen. Bak ham, fra retning av Thornhollow, begynner en klokke å kime — én gang for hver av dere — og tier, som om den har telt dere.

### 2. The Hollow Feast
**Setting:** Thornhollow's market square at dusk, tables laid under the black-apple trees.  
**Battlemap:** `ttb1-p5-l-village-streets` (Village Streets)  
**Beat:** The village has been expecting them. Reeve Mother Ilse denies Aelwen ever existed; the feast is black apples and grey bread; every wrist bears the Root's grey lichen ring. A child whispers Aelwen's name and is pulled away. Refusing the feast or the "guest quarters" brings the harvesters.  
**Enemies:**
*   `blighted-peasant` x3 (Existing, Minion)
*   `bog-witch` x1 (Existing, Final Boss — Reeve Mother Ilse)
*   `marsh-hound` x2 (Existing, Optional wave — the root-hounds Ilse calls from the orchards)

**Tactics:** Ilse opens with Curse of Bark to slow the heroes and Grasping Reeds to hold them still while the blighted peasants close in to spread Rotting Touch. Once below half HP, she calls the orchard's root-hounds as an optional wave.  
**Aftermath:** At 0 HP Ilse does not fall — she kneels, and the truth spills out: the village has fed the tree its *dying* for three hundred years to spare the young. "It is tired of the dying. This year it wants the children." The crypt-bell rings below the chapel.  
**Read Aloud:**
The village of Thornhollow waits for you at dusk, its houses leaning together like mourners. The streets are swept. The windows are lit. The air smells of cooking bread and of something underneath it, patient and sweet. In the market square, long tables have been laid — black apples in bowls of polished stone, grey loaves, pewter cups of cider that has gone to vinegar. Reeve Mother Ilse stands at the head of the table, her grey lichen ring bright on her wrist, and bids you welcome in a voice that has rehearsed this many times. Behind her, a child whispers a single name into the folds of her skirt — Aelwen — and is pulled away by an old woman's hand. No one has eaten a single bite.

**Read Aloud (Norwegian):**
Landsbyen Thornhollow venter på dere i skumringen, husene lener seg sammen som sørgende. Gatene er feid. Vinduene er tent. Luften lukter nystekt brød og noe under det — tålmodig og søtt. På torget er langbord dekket: svarte epler i skåler av polert stein, grå brød, tinnkrus med sider som har blitt til eddik. Årvarinne Ilse står ved bordenden med sin grå lavring lysende på håndleddet og ønsker dere velkommen i en stemme som har øvd på dette mange ganger. Bak henne hvisker et barn ett navn inn i skjørtets folder — Aelwen — og blir dratt vekk av en gammel kvinnes hånd. Ingen har spist en eneste bit.

### 3. The Unanswered Bell
**Setting:** The well-chapel: pews worn smooth by kneeling, a brass bell that tolls without a rope, a crypt-stair that breathes.  
**Battlemap:** `ttlb-p37-l-tomb` (Tomb)  
**Beat:** The chapel door opens to no one's touch. The priest who once tried to exorcise the tree guards the stair — hollowed now, his censer cold. The bell's tolling comes from below. The crypt stair is sealed by a bronze gate hung with three dirge-bells; an optional puzzle, The Reverse Dirge, lets the party open it silently by ringing the dirge backward (elder, bride, babe) — for the tree counts backward, feeding the eldest first. Wrong order rouses the guard and the dead below.  
**Optional Puzzle:** The Reverse Dirge — see scene notes. Fully optional; DMs may skip to combat, force the gate (DC 16 Strength), or pick it (DC 13 Thieves' Tools).  
**Enemies:**
*   `hollowed-acolyte` x1 (New, Undead)
*   `ghoul` x2 (Existing, Undead)

**Tactics:** The acolyte's Stench of the Chapel sickens those who close, and his Dreadful Glare pins a hero in place while the ghouls drag them toward the stair with Paralyzing Bite and Ravenous attacks.  
**Aftermath:** The acolyte, hollowed out, kneels at the top of the stair and does not rise. Behind it, the root-lined passage below hums in time with something massive.  
**Read Aloud:**
The chapel door opens to no one's touch. Inside, the pews are worn smooth by generations of kneeling, the floor stones hollowed where boots have stood. A brass bell hangs over the altar — and tolls, though no rope moves. The sound does not come from the bell. It comes from below, where a stone stair breathes in and out between the floorboards, slow as sleep. A figure stands at the head of the stair, robed and still, holding a censer that has not been swung in a long time. The priest who once tried to exorcise the tree. His eyes are grey. His mouth is open, and something roots inside it.

**Read Aloud (Norwegian):**
Kapelldøren åpner seg uten at noen rører den. Inne er kirkebenkene slitt glatte av generasjoners kneling, og gulvsteinene er hule der støvler har stått. En messingklokke henger over alteret — og klinger, selv om ingen snor rører seg. Lyden kommer ikke fra klokken. Den kommer nedenfra, der en steintrapp puster inn og ut mellom gulvplankene, langsomt som søvn. En skikkelse står ved trappens hode, kledd i kappe og urørlig, og holder et røkelsekar som ikke har blitt svingt på lenge. Presten som en gang prøvde å drive treet ut. Øynene hans er grå. Munnen hans er åpen, og noe slår rot inni den.

### 4. The Nursery of Rooted Things
**Setting:** Root-lined crypts where the village's sick and old are half-sunk into the packed earth.  
**Battlemap:** `ttb2-p27-l-cellars` (Cellars)  
**Beat:** The body-horror centerpiece. The rooted are aware, tended by Aelwen, who prunes them with gardener's shears to slow the tree's claim. She arms the party with the Heart-Knife. The children taken before she could save them stir.  
**Enemies:**
*   `rooted-child` x2 (New, Skirmisher)

**Tactics:** The rooted children crawl across walls and ceilings, weeping as they attack, poisoning with Weeping Sap. When one falls, its Last Weep stirs another rooted thing from the earth.  
**Aftermath:** Aelwen gives the Heart-Knife of black iron and speaks the truth: the Root holds a grey plague in its wood. Cut it and the plague seeps into the valley. Feed it and it sleeps. The choice belongs to whoever reaches the bottom of the well.  
**Read Aloud:**
The stair ends in a gallery of roots. Between the root-lined walls, bodies stand half-sunk in the packed earth like stones in a field — the sick and the old of Thornhollow, bark grown over their legs, their hands folded, black sap weeping from their eyes in slow, patient tears. They do not move. They breathe. Among them moves a woman in a midwife's apron, a pair of gardener's shears in her hand, pruning the roots that creep up their chests. She looks up at you, and her face is very tired. "You came," Sister Aelwen says, and for the first time the rooted things seem to lean toward the sound of a living voice.

**Read Aloud (Norwegian):**
Trappen ender i et galleri av røtter. Mellom de rotkledde veggene står kropper halvt sunket ned i den pakkede jorden som steiner i en åker — Thornhollows syke og gamle, med bark vokst over leggene, foldede hender, svart sevje som siver fra øynene i langsomme, tålmodige tårer. De beveger seg ikke. De puster. Blant dem beveger det seg en kvinne i jordmorforkle, med gartnersaks i hånden, som beskjærer røttene som kryper oppover brystene deres. Hun ser opp på dere, og ansiktet hennes er svært trøtt. "Dere kom," sier søster Aelwen, og for første gang synes de rotfaste å lene seg mot lyden av en levende stemme.

### 5. The Sap Galleries
**Setting:** The flooded root-works below the nursery, where grey sap runs through the tree's buried veins.  
**Battlemap:** `ttlb-p28-r-tunnel` (Tunnel)  
**Beat:** The mid-story twist: the "sick" the elders offered were never dying. The lichen rings are the tree's, pressed into every wrist at birth — it has been choosing its meals through them all along. The village's rebellion was the tree's harvest. The oldest harvests rise, and the Root-Warden guards the gate to the heart.  
**Enemies:**
*   `root-warden` x1 (New, Front line)
*   `zombie` x2 (Existing, Undead)

**Tactics:** The warden Engulfs the first hero through the gate and holds them while the zombies shove others into the sap pools, which are poison and difficult terrain. The warden regains 5 HP whenever it takes poison damage.  
**Aftermath:** The warden falls; the gallery opens into a flooded chamber lit from below by the grey pulse of the Heart.  
**Read Aloud:**
Below the nurseries, the roots thicken into galleries, and the galleries run with grey sap that moves upward, toward the village, in pulses like a heartbeat. Where the sap runs thickest, the walls are knotted with what look like roots — until you see the lichen rings: the same grey rings every villager wears, growing out of the stone, fed by the tree. The sick were never dying. The tree has been reaching up into the village for centuries, choosing its meals through the rings it pressed into their wrists at birth. And ahead, where the sap pools and the passage narrows, the roots themselves are standing up to meet you.

**Read Aloud (Norwegian):**
Under barnehagen tykner røttene til gallerier, og galleriene renner med grå sevje som beveger seg oppover, mot landsbyen, i pulser som et hjerteslag. Der sevjen renner tykkest, er veggene knuget av det som ser ut som røtter — helt til dere ser lavringene: de samme grå ringene som hver landsbyboer bærer, vokst ut av steinen, fôret av treet. De syke var aldri døende. Treet har nådd opp i landsbyen i århundrer, valgt sine måltider gjennom ringene det presset inn i håndleddene deres ved fødselen. Og foran, der sevjen samles og passasjen snevrer seg, reiser røttene seg for å møte dere.

### 6. The First Root
**Setting:** The flooded heart chamber, where the tree strangles the village's original wellstone.  
**Battlemap:** `ttlb-p32-v-underground-flooded` (Underground Flooded)  
**Beat:** The Root-Keeper — Thornhollow's first Reeve, fused to the tree for centuries — is courteous, sorrowful, and would rather the party take the ring than fight. The rooted children Aelwen failed to save rally to defend it.  
**Enemies:**
*   `root-keeper` x1 (New, Final Boss)
*   `rooted-child` x2 (New, Skirmisher)

**Tactics:** The Keeper offers the ring on the first round to any hero who approaches unarmed; it fights only when refused. Its Grey-Sap Curse shrinks hit point maxima and its Crown of Roots restrains the clustered. The rooted children harry the backline, weeping as they attack.  
**Aftermath:** At 0 HP the Keeper does not fall — it kneels, and the Heart is exposed, pulsing its slow, patient pulse. Aelwen arrives at the top of the stair with her shears. The choice is offered.  
**Read Aloud:**
The flooded chamber is lit from below. At its heart, the original wellstone of Thornhollow is wrapped in the tree's trunk like a fist around a bone, and from the stone the grey heart pulses — patient, older than the village, older than the valley. Before it stands a figure that was once a man, grown into the wood, his face a mask of bark and sorrow. He raises one root-woven hand in greeting, not in threat. "I was the first Reeve of this village," the Root-Keeper says, and his voice is the sound of water moving through roots. "I have kept the bargain so long I have forgotten the taste of sunlight. You will not like what the tree does when it is hungry. You will like even less what it does when it is dead."

**Read Aloud (Norwegian):**
Det oversvømte kammeret er opplyst nedenfra. I sentrum er Thornhollows opprinnelige brønnstein viklet inn i treets stamme som en knyttneve rundt et ben, og fra steinen pulserer det grå hjertet — tålmodig, eldre enn landsbyen, eldre enn dalen. Foran det står en skikkelse som en gang var et menneske, grodd inn i veden, ansiktet en maske av bark og sorg. Han løfter én rotvevd hånd til hilsen, ikke til trussel. "Jeg var denne landsbyens første fogd," sier rotvokteren, og stemmen hans er lyden av vann som beveger seg gjennom røtter. "Jeg har holdt pakten så lenge at jeg har glemt smaken av sollys. Dere vil ikke like hva treet gjør når det er sultent. Dere vil like enda mindre hva det gjør når det er dødt."

### 7. The Cut, the Torch, the Ring
**Setting:** The root chamber, the exposed Heart, the Keeper kneeling.  
**Beat:** The climax. No combat — a high-stakes decision. The Keeper kneels; the Heart is bare; the knife, the torch, and the ring lie ready. Three paths:
*   **The Cut (mercy):** Cut the Heart with the Heart-Knife. The tree dies, releasing the grey plague it held back. The rooted die at once, free. The valley sickens for a generation; Thornhollow empties. No feeding, ever again.
*   **The Torch (fire):** Burn the tree in its own chamber. The blight is burned from the wood, but the smoke quarantines Thornhollow for a generation. The Keeper dies feeling pain for the first time.
*   **The Ring (sacrifice):** One hero takes the Keeper's ring and becomes the new Keeper. The tree sleeps a hundred years; the village endures; the hero is lost to the daylight but goes smiling. The mercy bought with a life.
*   **The Bough (hidden path):** If Aelwen survived, the clean sapling hidden in her cottage can be grafted onto the Heart — the plague stays locked in the old wood, at the cost of a grey lichen ring on the grafter and a century of guarding the well.

**Aftermath:** The choice dictates the epilogue and the reward the survivors can offer.  
**Read Aloud:**
The Keeper kneels, and the grey heart is bare before you, pulsing its slow, patient pulse. The knife is in your hand. The torch is in your hand. The ring lies in the hollow where the Keeper's hand was, grey lichen coiling around it like a sleeping animal. Behind you, the stair breathes, and above it an entire village is holding its breath — a village that has fed a tree for centuries so that it would not have to feed on them. There is no right answer. There is only the one you can live with.

**Read Aloud (Norwegian):**
Vokteren kneler, og det grå hjertet ligger bart foran dere og pulserer sin langsomme, tålmodige puls. Kniven er i deres hånd. Fakkelen er i deres hånd. Ringen ligger i hulen der vokterens hånd var, med grå lav viklet rundt som et sovende dyr. Bak dere puster trappen, og over den holder en hel landsby pusten — en landsby som har fôret et tre i århundrer for at det ikke skulle fôre på dem. Det finnes ikke noe riktig svar. Bare det dere kan leve med.

---

## Epilogue

If the hero takes the ring: a hundred years of quiet. The village flourishes over a sleeping root, and the new Keeper is glimpsed, now and then, at the well — older than they should be, patient, smiling at the water. The elders gift the party the Grey Saint's Reliquary (+1 to saving throws against poison and disease).

If the Heart is cut: a grey summer settles on the valley. The rooted die quickly and painlessly in their galleries, and the freed dead are buried at the field's edge. The few survivors of Thornhollow give the party the reliquary and whatever they salvaged, weeping — not in sorrow, but in relief.

If the tree burns: ash and silence. The party leaves a smoking well and a village quarantined by the smoke for a generation. No reward but the knowledge that the blight is ended — and that the Keeper, at last, felt pain.
