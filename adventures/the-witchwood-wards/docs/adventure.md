# The Witchwood Wards

*A tabletop RPG adventure for 4 players, level 3–4, lasting 3–4 hours.*

---

## The Hook

The village of Coldwell lies ringed by the Witchwood — a blackwater swamp that
begins at the edge of its gardens and never ends. For generations the village
has been sealed inside a circle of five standing stones, and the witch who put
them there has been feeding on Coldwell's fear ever since.

Anyone who tries to leave is rooted to the spot. The feet sink first, then the
legs turn stiff and grey, bark creeping up the limbs until the wanderer stands
still as a tree at the road's edge — alive, aware, and unable to move. The last
messenger, a boy named Tam, made it to the bridge before the bark took his
knees. He clutches a letter from Elder Sable asking for help, and one hand is
already wood.

The stones are the wards. Break them, and the way to the witch is open.

## The Party's Objectives

- Reach Coldwell and answer the plea sealed in the bark.
- Break the ward circle and open the road through the Witchwood.
- Cross the blackwater marsh to the witch's den.
- Face the Bog Witch and decide what the curse deserves.

---

## The Storyline

### 1. The Rooted Road
The party finds Tam at the edge of the marsh — half-barked, still breathing,
holding Elder Sable's letter. The bark creeps as he speaks, and it does not
hurt him anymore; it is what the curse takes from you last, hope. Coldwell
waits past the bridge, crowded into its ring of stones. The villagers are
kind, frightened, and convinced the witch wants only their fear. Elder Sable
tells the legend: a witch, a broken promise, and a village sealed for her
hunger. She asks the heroes to walk the road she cannot.

### 2. The Blackwater Crossing
Beyond the ward ring the road sinks into the swamp. The water is black and
still; hummocks of moss shift underfoot, and pale lights dance between the
alders — a will-o'-wisp or two, drifting toward the slowest walker. The witch's
court hunts here: marsh hounds that drag their prey into the mud and hold it
there. The crossing is a fight in stages, over sucking pools and through
reeds that grab at the legs, until the five standing stones loom out of the
fog.

### 3. The Ward Circle
The stones stand in a ring on a drowned meadow, each carved with a symbol: a
leaf, a flame, a wave, a wind, a stone. Set into the ring's center is a
battered shrine of the founder of Coldwell — a woman named Elsweth, whose
statue still holds out a wooden cup. The order of the symbols is written in
the shrine's carvings and on the moss-eaten tablets around it: the founder
served her village wind, fire, water, leaf, and stone — the order in which a
seedling grows, and the order in which the wards must be pressed.

Press them in the right order and the ring shatters. Press one wrong, and a
ward spirit rises from the mud to hold the presser down until the circle is
quiet again. Trial and error works, but each mistake costs blood and time.

### 4. The Witch's Den
Where the fifth stone stood, the ground sags into a hollow beneath the roots
of a drowned oak — the witch's den, warm and dry, hung with the things the
swamp has swallowed: bells, boots, a child's wooden toys. Her bark-touched
servants stand like furniture until the heroes enter, and the marsh hounds
come at her call. The witch fights with the cold patience of the bog — but she
is old, and the heroes are not.

### 5. The Broken Promise
When the witch falls, she does not die. She kneels in the mire and the truth
spills out: she is Elsweth, the founder whose shrine the villagers still keep —
betrayed in the founding days, sealed in the swamp by the people she saved,
and cursed to feed on their fear for as long as the wards stood. The curse is
not her hatred; it is her grief, made flesh and rooted in the mud. Her death
will lift the wards and set the barked free — but she asks, before she goes,
whether the village's children will remember her as the monster or the founder.

The heroes choose. If they grant her peace — a word, a prayer, her wooden cup
returned to her hands — the bark falls from Tam and the other rooted folk like
shed skin, and the swamp drains quiet. If they strike her down in anger, the
wards break all the same, but the Witchwood keeps its cold edge, and the
village will tell the story of a monster killed by heroes who chose to hate.

### Epilogue
The road from Coldwell is open for the first time in generations. The village
gives the heroes what it has: the founder's relics from the den — her heirloom,
an heirloom of their choosing, and the hope of a village that can finally walk
out of the ring of stones.
