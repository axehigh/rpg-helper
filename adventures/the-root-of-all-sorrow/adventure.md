# Intro

A letter in a shaking hand arrives with the last courier to cross the fens: Sister Aelwen, village midwife and an old friend of one of the heroes, begs the party to reach Thornhollow before the full moon.
"They mean to choose this year. Not the dying — the young. When the bell rings on the full moon, the tree will take them. Come quickly, and do not eat the black apples."
No traveler has returned from Thornhollow in years, and no one who has worn the grey lichen ring has ever left it. You must cross the fens, reach the village before the moon swells full, and stop the tree from being fed.

# Objectives

- Reach Thornhollow before the full moon and learn what the elders feed the tree beneath the well-chapel.
- Follow the crypt-bell through the chapel and find Sister Aelwen among the rooted.
- Descend the sap galleries to the First Root and confront the Root-Keeper.
- Choose the fate of the Root — and of Thornhollow.

# Scene: The Black-Apple Road

![The Black-Apple Road](adventures/the-root-of-all-sorrow/images/01_black_apple_road.jpg)

- id: scene-1
- battlemap: ttlb-p3-l-country-lane

## Read Aloud

The road into the valley narrows between hedgerows heavy with black apples, each fruit swollen and glossy, speckled with grey.
Where the hedgerows break, you pass a drowned hamlet — a millwheel still turning in a pond that smells of copper, a bell-crier's house with its clapper stolen.
At the foot of a great tree that has split a mass grave open, a boy sits cross-legged, drawing in the mud.
He does not look up when you approach. He is drawing a root, over and over.
Behind him, from the direction of Thornhollow, a bell begins to toll — once for each of you — and falls silent, as if it has counted you.

## Read Aloud (Norsk)

Veien inn i dalen smalner mellom hekker som er tunge av svarte epler — hvert frukt hovent og blankt, prikket med grått.
Der hekkene brytes, passerer dere en druknet landsby: et møllehjul som fremdeles går rundt i en dam som lukter kobber, og et ringerves hus med klokken stjålet.
Ved foten av et stort tre som har splittet en massegrav åpen, sitter en gutt med korslagte ben og tegner i gjørmen.
Han ser ikke opp når dere nærmer dere. Han tegner en rot, om og om igjen.
Bak ham, fra retning av Thornhollow, begynner en klokke å kime — én gang for hver av dere — og tier, som om den har telt dere.

## Environment

The fen road narrows between black-apple hedgerows. The mill pond is poisoned (drinking from it requires a DC 11 Constitution save or 1d4 poison damage). The root-hounds do not chase beyond the grove line.

## Enemies

- marsh-hound x2
- marsh-hound x1 (optional)

## DM Notes

- Benle the Mute: a youth who fled Thornhollow a year ago. He knows the well-chapel's stair and draws roots in the mud — he is a guide if the party keeps him. He touches his wrist where the lichen ring would be.
- The Counted Bell: the chapel bell tolls once per hero. The village knows they are coming.
- The Root-Hounds: mangy orchard dogs, re-flavored marsh hounds, marked with grey lichen. They drag prey toward the nearest black-apple tree.
- The Midwife's Badge: a torn corpse by the drowned mill bears Aelwen's midwife badge — or a copy planted by the tree's servants.

## Tactics & Synergy

- Pack Hunt: the root-hounds circle the party, using Muddy Grip to grapple a hero, then dragging them toward the grove where the roots are hungry.
- Grove Line: the hounds break off pursuit if the party flees past the hedgerow — they will not leave the tree's shadow.

## Aftermath & Clues

The torn corpse at the drowned mill wears Aelwen's midwife badge. Benle draws the well-chapel in the mud, then touches his wrist. The chapel bell has counted you — the village is waiting.

# Scene: The Hollow Feast

![The Hollow Feast](adventures/the-root-of-all-sorrow/images/02_hollow_feast.jpg)

- id: scene-2
- battlemap: ttb1-p5-l-village-streets

## Read Aloud

The village of Thornhollow waits for you at dusk, its houses leaning together like mourners.
The streets are swept. The windows are lit. The air smells of cooking bread and of something underneath it, patient and sweet.
In the market square, long tables have been laid — black apples in bowls of polished stone, grey loaves, pewter cups of cider that has gone to vinegar.
Reeve Mother Ilse stands at the head of the table, her grey lichen ring bright on her wrist, and bids you welcome in a voice that has rehearsed this many times.
Behind her, a child whispers a single name into the folds of her skirt — Aelwen — and is pulled away by an old woman's hand.
No one has eaten a single bite.

## Read Aloud (Norsk)

Landsbyen Thornhollow venter på dere i skumringen, husene lener seg sammen som sørgende.
Gatene er feid. Vinduene er tent. Luften lukter nystekt brød og noe under det — tålmodig og søtt.
På torget er langbord dekket: svarte epler i skåler av polert stein, grå brød, tinnkrus med sider som har blitt til eddik.
Årvarinne Ilse står ved bordenden med sin grå lavring lysende på håndleddet og ønsker dere velkommen i en stemme som har øvd på dette mange ganger.
Bak henne hvisker et barn ett navn inn i skjørtets folder — Aelwen — og blir dratt vekk av en gammel kvinnes hånd.
Ingen har spist en eneste bit.

## Environment

Thornhollow's market square at dusk, tables laid under the black-apple trees. The streets are swept clean; nothing grows in the village itself. The root-cellar beneath the hall is the "guest quarters."

## Enemies

- blighted-peasant x3
- bog-witch
- marsh-hound x2 (optional)

## DM Notes

- Mother Ilse, Reeve: a bog-witch holding the village's bargain for a generation. Her lichen ring is bright — the tree favors her. She is courteous and dangerous.
- The Feast: black apples and grey bread. Eating the apples means taking the tree's ring. Do not eat the black apples.
- The Whisper: a child knows Aelwen is in the crypts. Asked, Ilse says, "There has been no midwife here in thirty years."
- The Harvest: if the party refuses the feast or the guest quarters, the blighted harvesters come. Ilse fights while the peasants close in; at 0 HP she kneels and confesses the bargain.
- Root-Hound Call: once below half HP, Ilse calls the orchard's root-hounds (optional wave of 2 marsh hounds).

## Tactics & Synergy

- Curse of Bark: Ilse opens by slowing a hero and taxing them with necrotic damage each turn.
- Grasping Reeds: Ilse restrains the melee line so the blighted peasants can close and spread Rotting Touch.
- Joyous Burst: remind melee combatants that a peasant at 0 HP explodes in a shower of putrid broth.
- Broken Promise: when Ilse drops to 0 HP she does not fall — she kneels, and the truth spills out.

## Aftermath & Clues

Ilse kneels, and the truth spills out: the village has fed the tree its dying for three hundred years to spare the young. "It is tired of the dying. This year it wants the children." Below the chapel, the crypt-bell rings.

# Scene: The Unanswered Bell

![The Unanswered Bell](adventures/the-root-of-all-sorrow/images/03_unanswered_bell.jpg)

- id: scene-3
- battlemap: ttlb-p37-l-tomb

## Read Aloud

The chapel door opens to no one's touch.
Inside, the pews are worn smooth by generations of kneeling, the floor stones hollowed where boots have stood.
A brass bell hangs over the altar — and tolls, though no rope moves.
The sound does not come from the bell. It comes from below, where a stone stair breathes in and out between the floorboards, slow as sleep.
A figure stands at the head of the stair, robed and still, holding a censer that has not been swung in a long time.
The priest who once tried to exorcise the tree. His eyes are grey. His mouth is open, and something roots inside it.

## Read Aloud (Norsk)

Kapelldøren åpner seg uten at noen rører den.
Inne er kirkebenkene slitt glatte av generasjoners kneling, og gulvsteinene er hule der støvler har stått.
En messingklokke henger over alteret — og klinger, selv om ingen snor rører seg.
Lyden kommer ikke fra klokken. Den kommer nedenfra, der en steintrapp puster inn og ut mellom gulvplankene, langsomt som søvn.
En skikkelse står ved trappens hode, kledd i kappe og urørlig, og holder et røkelsekar som ikke har blitt svingt på lenge.
Presten som en gang prøvde å drive treet ut. Øynene hans er grå. Munnen hans er åpen, og noe slår rot inni den.

## Environment

The well-chapel: pews worn smooth by kneeling, a brass bell that tolls without a rope, a crypt-stair that breathes. The bell's sound comes from below.

## Puzzle (Optional)

The crypt stair is sealed by a bronze gate hung with three bells, each engraved with one line of the village's burial dirge. Across the gate's lintel the dirge is spelled out: "One for the babe who never woke, two for the bride who choked on the apple, three for the elder who fed the tree." Ringing the bells in the dirge's order wakes the hollowed acolyte and the dead below. Ringing them in reverse — elder, bride, babe — opens the gate silently, for the tree counts backward: it feeds the eldest first, and the village has always buried its chosen oldest-first to buy time for the young. A hero who studies the gate with Insight or History may recognize that the tree numbers the dead the other way around.

## Enemies

- hollowed-acolyte
- ghoul x2

## DM Notes

- The Unanswered Bell: tolls of its own accord, counting the party. The sound comes from below, not from the bell.
- The Hollowed Acolyte: the priest who once tried to exorcise the tree. He guards the crypt stair, his censer cold. He attacks only if the party touches the stair — otherwise he weeps, in dust.
- The Crypt Stair: breathes in time with something massive below.
- Optional Puzzle — The Reverse Dirge: fully optional. Skip it and fight the acolyte and ghouls, force the gate (DC 16 Strength), pick it (DC 13 Thieves' Tools), or solve the dirge. Wrong order rouses the guard and the dead below; right order (elder, bride, babe) opens the stair in silence and the acolyte never stirs.

## Tactics & Synergy

- Stench of the Chapel: creatures that close with the acolyte sicken unless they pass a DC 11 Constitution save.
- Dreadful Glare: the acolyte pins a hero in place with fear while the ghouls close.
- Ghoul Drag: the ghouls use Paralyzing Bite on the pinned hero, then Ravenous attacks to drag them toward the stair.

## Aftermath & Clues

The hollowed acolyte kneels at the top of the stair and does not rise. Behind it, the root-lined passage below hums in time with something massive.

# Scene: The Nursery of Rooted Things

![The Nursery of Rooted Things](adventures/the-root-of-all-sorrow/images/04_nursery.jpg)

- id: scene-4
- battlemap: ttb2-p27-l-cellars

## Read Aloud

The stair ends in a gallery of roots.
Between the root-lined walls, bodies stand half-sunk in the packed earth like stones in a field — the sick and the old of Thornhollow, bark grown over their legs, their hands folded, black sap weeping from their eyes in slow, patient tears.
They do not move. They breathe.
Among them moves a woman in a midwife's apron, a pair of gardener's shears in her hand, pruning the roots that creep up their chests.
She looks up at you, and her face is very tired. "You came," Sister Aelwen says, and for the first time the rooted things seem to lean toward the sound of a living voice.

## Read Aloud (Norsk)

Trappen ender i et galleri av røtter.
Mellom de rotkledde veggene står kropper halvt sunket ned i den pakkede jorden som steiner i en åker — Thornhollows syke og gamle, med bark vokst over leggene, foldede hender, svart sevje som siver fra øynene i langsomme, tålmodige tårer.
De beveger seg ikke. De puster.
Blant dem beveger det seg en kvinne i jordmorforkle, med gartnersaks i hånden, som beskjærer røttene som kryper oppover brystene deres.
Hun ser opp på dere, og ansiktet hennes er svært trøtt. "Dere kom," sier søster Aelwen, og for første gang synes de rotfaste å lene seg mot lyden av en levende stemme.

## Environment

Root-lined crypts where the village's sick and old are half-sunk into the packed earth. The walls breathe. Black sap drips from the ceiling in slow, patient tears.

## Enemies

- rooted-child x2

## DM Notes

- Aelwen: the party's contact, the village midwife. She has been pruning the rooted for months to slow the tree's claim — a holding action, not a cure.
- The Rooted: the village's sick and old, fed to the tree for generations. Aware, breathing, weeping sap. The elders' secret rebellion — they fed the dying to spare the young.
- The Rooted Children: the ones taken before Aelwen could save them. They attack weeping — they are the tree's now.
- The Heart-Knife: black iron, given by Aelwen. One clean cut at the heart. She has not had the strength to go down herself.
- The Truth: the Root holds a grey plague in its wood. Cut it and the plague seeps into the valley. Feed it and it sleeps. The choice belongs to whoever reaches the bottom of the well.

## Tactics & Synergy

- Rooted Crawl: the children crawl across walls and ceilings, attacking from the dark, avoiding the floor where the rooted stand.
- Weeping Sap: each hit poisons unless the target passes a DC 11 Constitution save.
- Last Weep: when a child falls, its wail stirs the crypts — at the start of the next round, one rooted creature crawls forth.

## Aftermath & Clues

Aelwen gives the Heart-Knife of black iron and speaks the truth of the Root. The rooted weep silently as the party passes. The passage below hums louder.

# Scene: The Sap Galleries

![The Sap Galleries](adventures/the-root-of-all-sorrow/images/05_sap_galleries.jpg)

- id: scene-5
- battlemap: ttlb-p28-r-tunnel

## Read Aloud

Below the nurseries, the roots thicken into galleries, and the galleries run with grey sap that moves upward, toward the village, in pulses like a heartbeat.
Where the sap runs thickest, the walls are knotted with what look like roots — until you see the lichen rings: the same grey rings every villager wears, growing out of the stone, fed by the tree.
The sick were never dying. The tree has been reaching up into the village for centuries, choosing its meals through the rings it pressed into their wrists at birth.
And ahead, where the sap pools and the passage narrows, the roots themselves are standing up to meet you.

## Read Aloud (Norsk)

Under barnehagen tykner røttene til gallerier, og galleriene renner med grå sevje som beveger seg oppover, mot landsbyen, i pulser som et hjerteslag.
Der sevjen renner tykkest, er veggene knuget av det som ser ut som røtter — helt til dere ser lavringene: de samme grå ringene som hver landsbyboer bærer, vokst ut av steinen, fôret av treet.
De syke var aldri døende. Treet har nådd opp i landsbyen i århundrer, valgt sine måltider gjennom ringene det presset inn i håndleddene deres ved fødselen.
Og foran, der sevjen samles og passasjen snevrer seg, reiser røttene seg for å møte dere.

## Environment

The flooded root-works below the nursery. Grey sap runs upward toward the village in pulses. Sap pools are difficult terrain and poison to drink (DC 11 Constitution save or 1d4 poison damage).

## Enemies

- root-warden
- zombie x2

## DM Notes

- The Twist: the "sick" the elders offered were never dying. The lichen rings are the tree's, pressed at birth — it has been choosing its meals through them all along. The village's rebellion was the tree's harvest.
- The Sap Galleries: grey sap flows upward, toward the village. Difficult terrain; the sap is poison to drink.
- The Root-Warden: the tree's bodyguard, rooted in the pool. It Engulfs the first hero through the gate.
- The Oldest Harvests: zombies risen from the tree's feeding pits, wearing lichen rings on rotted wrists.

## Tactics & Synergy

- Engulf: the warden swallows the first hero through the gate, grappling and restraining them while the zombies shove others into the sap pools.
- Sap Regeneration: the warden regains 5 HP whenever it takes poison damage — poison attacks feed it.
- Undead Fortitude: the zombies are hard to put down; remind the party to burn them or focus fire.

## Aftermath & Clues

The warden falls, and the gallery opens into a flooded chamber lit from below by the grey pulse of the Heart.

# Scene: The First Root

![The First Root](adventures/the-root-of-all-sorrow/images/06_first_root.jpg)

- id: scene-6
- battlemap: ttlb-p32-v-underground-flooded

## Read Aloud

The flooded chamber is lit from below.
At its heart, the original wellstone of Thornhollow is wrapped in the tree's trunk like a fist around a bone, and from the stone the grey heart pulses — patient, older than the village, older than the valley.
Before it stands a figure that was once a man, grown into the wood, his face a mask of bark and sorrow.
He raises one root-woven hand in greeting, not in threat.
"I was the first Reeve of this village," the Root-Keeper says, and his voice is the sound of water moving through roots.
"I have kept the bargain so long I have forgotten the taste of sunlight. You will not like what the tree does when it is hungry. You will like even less what it does when it is dead."

## Read Aloud (Norsk)

Det oversvømte kammeret er opplyst nedenfra.
I sentrum er Thornhollows opprinnelige brønnstein viklet inn i treets stamme som en knyttneve rundt et ben, og fra steinen pulserer det grå hjertet — tålmodig, eldre enn landsbyen, eldre enn dalen.
Foran det står en skikkelse som en gang var et menneske, grodd inn i veden, ansiktet en maske av bark og sorg.
Han løfter én rotvevd hånd til hilsen, ikke til trussel.
"Jeg var denne landsbyens første fogd," sier rotvokteren, og stemmen hans er lyden av vann som beveger seg gjennom røtter.
"Jeg har holdt pakten så lenge at jeg har glemt smaken av sollys. Dere vil ikke like hva treet gjør når det er sultent. Dere vil like enda mindre hva det gjør når det er dødt."

## Environment

The flooded heart chamber, where the tree strangles the village's original wellstone. The grey Heart pulses from the stone. The chamber floor is deep, cold water up to the knees.

## Enemies

- root-keeper
- rooted-child x2

## DM Notes

- The Root-Keeper: Thornhollow's first Reeve, fused to the tree for centuries. Courteous, sorrowful, patient. It offers the ring before it fights.
- The Grey Heart: the exposed wellstone wrapped in the trunk. Cutting it releases the plague; feeding it buys sleep.
- The Rooted Children: Aelwen's failures rally to defend the tree, weeping as they attack.
- Patient Grief: at 0 HP the Keeper does not fall — it kneels, and the choice is offered.

## Tactics & Synergy

- The Offer: if a hero approaches unarmed on the first round, the Keeper kneels and holds out the ring. It fights only when refused.
- Grey-Sap Curse: each hit shrinks the target's hit point maximum until a long rest — track it, and let the party feel the tree's patience.
- Crown of Roots: the Keeper restrains clustered heroes, then the rooted children harry the backline.

## Aftermath & Clues

The Keeper kneels, and the Heart is exposed, pulsing its slow, patient pulse. Aelwen arrives at the top of the stair with her shears. The choice is offered.

# Scene: The Cut, the Torch, the Ring

![The Cut, the Torch, the Ring](adventures/the-root-of-all-sorrow/images/07_the_choice.jpg)

- id: scene-7

## Read Aloud

The Keeper kneels, and the grey heart is bare before you, pulsing its slow, patient pulse.
The knife is in your hand. The torch is in your hand. The ring lies in the hollow where the Keeper's hand was, grey lichen coiling around it like a sleeping animal.
Behind you, the stair breathes, and above it an entire village is holding its breath — a village that has fed a tree for centuries so that it would not have to feed on them.
There is no right answer. There is only the one you can live with.

## Read Aloud (Norsk)

Vokteren kneler, og det grå hjertet ligger bart foran dere og pulserer sin langsomme, tålmodige puls.
Kniven er i deres hånd. Fakkelen er i deres hånd. Ringen ligger i hulen der vokterens hånd var, med grå lav viklet rundt som et sovende dyr.
Bak dere puster trappen, og over den holder en hel landsby pusten — en landsby som har fôret et tre i århundrer for at det ikke skulle fôre på dem.
Det finnes ikke noe riktig svar. Bare det dere kan leve med.

## Environment

The root chamber, the exposed Heart, the Keeper kneeling. The water is still. The sap has stopped running.

## DM Notes

- Option 1 — The Cut (mercy): cut the Heart with the Heart-Knife. The tree dies, releasing the grey plague it held back. The rooted die at once, free. The valley sickens for a generation; Thornhollow empties. No feeding, ever again.
- Option 2 — The Torch (fire): burn the tree in its own chamber. The blight is burned from the wood, but the smoke quarantines Thornhollow for a generation. The Keeper dies feeling pain for the first time.
- Option 3 — The Ring (sacrifice): one hero takes the Keeper's ring and becomes the new Keeper. The tree sleeps a hundred years; the village endures; the hero is lost to the daylight but goes smiling.
- Option 4 — The Bough (hidden): if Aelwen survived, the clean sapling hidden in her cottage can be grafted onto the Heart — the plague stays locked in the old wood, at the cost of a grey lichen ring on the grafter and a century of guarding the well.

## Aftermath & Clues

The choice is made, and the sap falls still. Above, Thornhollow breathes out.
If the ring was taken, the elders gift the Grey Saint's Reliquary (+1 to saving throws against poison and disease), and the village remembers.
If the Heart was cut, the few survivors give the reliquary and whatever they salvaged, weeping in relief.
If the tree burns, the party leaves a smoking well — no reward but the knowledge the blight is ended.
